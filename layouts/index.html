The route so far

Kitchen, caravan, mountain, laptop.

  1. IndiaBasileia

    A skincare brand that started with bad skin.

    Nothing on the market worked for my skin, so I learned formulation and made my own. First for me, then friends, then family, who became a reluctant testing department.

    It became Basileia: fifteen hand-formulated products, a registered trademark, and gift hampers in five countries, all through word of mouth.

    When I moved to Dubai, I handed the stock, formulas and equipment to another founder. Basileia didn't end. It just stopped being mine.

  2. DubaiHappy Zoned

    Building the caravan was the easy part.

    Happy Zoned was caravan sleeping pods for budget stays. I designed the units myself, down to where the water tanks, the generator and the tow hitch go.

    Happy Zoned prices and costs
    A bunk, per nightAED 50
    The whole caravan, per weekAED 5,000
    Building one unit~AED 24,000

    The numbers worked. The paperwork didn't. Mobile housing had no neat category in GCC zoning, so I shelved it. New Zealand and Australia are friendlier to this kind of thing.

  3. The HimalayasTime off

    I went to the Himalayas for the silence. Then I kept moving.

    After Basileia and Happy Zoned I took a few months off. I rented a quiet place in the mountains, mostly to remember what silence sounded like.

    The stillness had an odd effect: it made me never want to fully stop moving again.

  4. Southeast AsiaYou are here

    A planner that works like paper.

    Japanese paper planner brands are talking about licensing it.

    I started FlowPlanned while travelling with a paper planner in one bag and a phone full of half-used apps in the other. Neither talked to the other. So I built one thing that does both jobs, vibe-coding it piece by piece until it stopped feeling like a compromise.

    People who make paper already understand why software should work offline, so those conversations are short.

    If you want to know when it's out, write to me.

    On the side

    Radios that work where phones don't.

    I'm building LoRaWAN sensor projects and reading a lot about solar. LoRaWAN is long range and low power, and it works where there's no coverage and no plan to pay for.

    What I'm figuring out is how to keep small devices powered and talking to each other with neither.

    I work on this independently, so the funding comes from people who want it to exist. Chip in with crypto.
