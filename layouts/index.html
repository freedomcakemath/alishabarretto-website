Now building

A planner that works like paper

Japanese paper planner brands are talking about licensing it.

Southeast Asia. FlowPlanned is a local planner for your PC that behaves like a paper notebook. It runs on your machine, the AI too, and it never asks you to sign up.

I started it while travelling with a paper planner in one bag and a phone full of half-used apps in the other. Neither talked to the other. So I built one thing that does both jobs, vibe-coding it piece by piece until it stopped feeling like a compromise.

{{ partial "photo.html" (dict "src" "/images/image_2-360.webp" "w" 360 "h" 270 "alt" "Planning FlowPlanned in a paper planner." "caption" "Where it started: planning FlowPlanned on paper") }}

I'm now talking to Japanese paper planner brands about licensing it. People who make paper already understand why software should work offline, so those conversations are short.

If you want to know when it's out, write to me.

Research

Radios that work where phones don't

I'm also building LoRaWAN sensor projects and reading a lot about solar. LoRaWAN is long range and low power, and it works where there's no coverage and no plan to pay for. What I'm figuring out is how to keep small devices powered and talking to each other with neither.

The feature

I went to the Himalayas for the silence. Then I kept moving.

After Basileia and the Happy Zoned attempt I took a few months off. I rented a quiet place in the Himalayas, mostly to remember what silence sounded like.

The stillness had an odd effect: it made me never want to fully stop moving again. So I went to Southeast Asia, and I'm still here, working from whichever city or island has decent wifi that week. FlowPlanned got built somewhere in the middle of all that.
