Everyone asks whether AI is a boon or a bane. This will be a never-ending, long discussion that can potentially even lead to heated arguments. Since the inception of COVID, specifically 2020, we’ve seen significant changes in society and how people operate. There has been a rapid change in how tech teams operate, the focus shifted from what to deliver to how fast to deliver. Of course, speed in innovation matters a lot, and we saw how LLMs helped bridge that gap. These are indeed exciting times to be living in. What would earlier take a month can now be done in a week or a few days, at the least. With the right guidance from people with expertise, wonders can take place. It’s as if we’re outside the realm of time.

While some people are completely into it, working on various projects broadly, others prefer to stay away entirely, anti-AI, with strict rules about AI usage in their orgs, while some others believe in human-led, AI-assisted work. Imagine a small kid, if you leave them on the road in the middle of nowhere, they’ll have no direction and will wander aimlessly, clueless. It’s the same with AI, it needs to be led, it needs to be governed with strict rules.

I’ve seen some orgs put good rules in place around AI usage. Education around this topic is crucial. Awareness, paired with action-driven outcomes, is key.

This lack of governance has real consequences. I came across people talking about getting their crypto wallets drained because they downloaded some “trading bot” code that promised to automate trading and make them thousands, if not millions, of dollars every month. Delusion at its peak. People didn't get scammed by AI, they got scammed by their own desire to skip the "how" and jump straight to the outcome. If we use AI blindly, this is bound to happen, it’s the same with anything else in the world.

The risk isn't just individual either. We're also seeing the early shape of AI agents going up against each other, automated trading systems reacting to other automated trading systems, at speeds no human is actually watching in real time. One algorithm outsmarting another can ripple into markets way beyond just those two systems. And then there's the sheer amount of money being poured into AI infrastructure, compute, chips, data centers, a lot of it financed through debt, not cash on hand. Going forward, we need to be more cautious, more careful, and more vigilant about our decisions, not just as individuals, but in how we let these systems, and this much financial exposure, interact with each other.

Think about this, what if everyone in the world looked the same, dressed the same, spoke the same way, and were 100% identical? That’d be so boring. Even when we come across purely AI-generated content, what’s now called “AI slop,” we can instantly identify it. In the past, I’d give my writing to AI to grammar-check or polish, and it would completely change and strip out the essence, the “life-giving” factor, making it dead and lifeless and same-y. It’s telling that sites now offer a “human score” for how human generated content feels, and people are loving it.

AI can definitely not replace anyone unless you allow it to replace you. Your uniqueness, your personality, and your creative mind are your power in these times.
