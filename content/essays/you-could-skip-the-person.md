These days I’ve been thinking about one particular topic a lot. There was a time when everything was run based on relationships and trust. If you needed something, you could get it from someone you had a relationship with. For example, something as simple as grocery shopping. There were no stores hundreds of years ago, people would trade with people they knew. So that means they had to put in efforts and nurture their relationships with people. But today, the definition of community changes. Even if the word community is still being used, community began to water down right there.

It wasn’t like this even just two generations ago. Consider this, a family unit, then a neighbourhood. A community and a community of communities. Imagine this multiplying to where the entire nation is like this. And every exchange was tracked in their memory, they didn’t even need on-chain blockchain back then.

I recall this happened in the vicinity of my grandmother’s. When a child was ill, someone came to the doorstep of that family with a bowl of soup without even asking. This is how we did it in the day. People watched, acted and really helped and supported each other. They didn’t do it for the gram. Back then, if you broke your word, the word about you would spread fast. This is what people tend to forget when they get nostalgic about the good old days.

Today’s generation has more things than the generation of our grandparents. We own more material possessions, but we have less warmth. Things have taken the place of people in our lives. The power of tech today is so powerful we can talk to someone in a completely different part of the world in another timezone. But no bonding, more communication but no bonding. More stuff, but no connection.

Have you ever met someone who doesn’t speak your language but you both managed to convey what you had to, and both understood each other like telepathy, so language is also never a barrier. Thoughts go from your mind to the receiver’s mind somehow and they understand it in a way they can. It’s the inner workings of a human mind, how they view people. We all need to pitch in and work for the greater good.

Last month I had a delivery at my door. I heard the bike before I saw him. He arrived with the bag in one hand and the phone in the other, screen bright with my name as a pin on a map. We communicated in maybe 2-3 words, he asked whether that was my name and I nodded and agreed. That was it, he handed the parcel to me and left swiftly, already seeing where he has to go next to deliver the next parcel. Neither of us had to become anyone to the other. I didn’t even have the opportunity to offer him any water, it was all too fast. This is the fast-paced life we all are used to.

Paper notes did not appear in one clean year. China had them early. Europe played with them later. What changed as the 1800s wore on is how ordinary they became. More states printed them and more shops adopted them. More strangers could close a deal without trusting a name. Before that a lot of daily life still ran on a different kind of credit. It was about the relationship. Whether you helped when it was ugly outside. It was barter, a value of exchange based on trust, respect and harmony. A printed rectangle let you skip that. Just hand it over and leave, the human connection became optional.

Once the optional path exists, people take it. Why spend months building a tie if a note can finish the job by evening. The groups that would run on these principles start running on payment. On the outside, it may still seem to be everything genuine community driven but the practice underneath is a till.

Marx watched a harsher version of the same split. Owners starting to hoard more. The people who were the actual makers falling behind. He thought that stretch would snap, pass through a long in-between, and land in a society with no state and no money. Enough of everything that greed and envy would have less to feed on. What later called itself communism kept the state and kept the cash. The name traveled. The destination did not. He still named something true. People stop meeting as people and start meeting as sides.

Money did not invent the cold part in us. It gave that part a tool that works at distance. You can take and not sit with the face for long. You start to feel slightly owed for every decent act. You start to feel slightly suspicious of any help that does not come with a rate.

Imagine contributing what is within us for the greater good of the community not for selfish private gains. Think of it in a bigger way, if the community you are part of is thriving, you also thrive along with it, you become as a result of the success of the community, it is a by result. Hence always think bigger.

The same is repeating at a larger scale now. Agents doing various tasks without sharing a meal with anyone. Currencies floating on debts too large to picture. People talk about agent wars and the future of money as if those are only technical plots. They are also a test.

A world that only knows how to transact will keep making full rooms and thin days. The part that holds, if it holds, will come from people who still let help live in memory. Work that is not only a transfer. A face that is not a checkout.

The future will not belong to the pile of notes. It will belong to the communities that can still thrive when there is no receipt for the decent thing.
